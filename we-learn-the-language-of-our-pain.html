We Learn the Language of Our Pain

Reflections on Family Estrangement

By Mani S. Kavuru

Once upon a simpler time, When family quarrels weren't a crime, We fought, we sulked, we slammed a door, Then came back home to fight some more. But something shifted, something changed, And suddenly we're all estranged. A careless word, a slight recalled, A bridge once crossed, now firmly walled. We learn the language of our pain, But not the way to love again. A TikTok sage, in sixty seconds, Pronounces judgment, never reckons. "Your mother's toxic!" comes the cry, "Your father's narcissistic!"—goodbye! And therapists, though often wise, May see the world through wounded eyes. One side is heard, the other gone, And silence quietly marches on. We learn the language of our pain, But not the way to love again. Of course, some homes conceal a hell, Of cruelties too dark to tell. But must each ordinary slight Become a lifelong wrong to right? A brother lost, a sister gone, A birthday passes; life moves on. The empty chair, the silent phone, A family tree with branches lone. And parents, too, must own their part, The careless words that pierced a heart. For love alone cannot erase The hurt reflected in a face. Yet forgiveness is a higher art, The quiet courage of the heart. Not to forget, nor wrongs defend, But choose compassion over end. For being right may soothe our pride, While years of love are cast aside. And mercy, freely given, may Restore what anger swept away. We learn the language of our pain, But can we learn to love again? We call it healing, call it growth, But is it always truly both? When boundaries become a wall, Who wins—and what becomes of all? And time, that thief who never waits, Moves quietly through bolted gates. One day the phone will never ring, No chance to mend a broken thing. For love was never meant to be A guarantee of harmony. Perhaps its bravest words are true: "I'm flawed, and so, my dear, are you." And when the final years remain, May grace prevail where pride has reigned. For greater than the need to win Is finding room to let love in.
